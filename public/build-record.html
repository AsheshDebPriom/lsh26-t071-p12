R4Savings pockets, with dates from the forecast

Name, item, target and monthly contribution. The completion date comes from running the calendar forward, month by month, handing each pocket only the surplus the forecast predicts, minus whatever the pockets ahead of it already took. When a month is short the contribution is capped and the date slips.

Each card prints the naive target ÷ contribution figure beside the simulated one, so the difference is visible rather than claimed. A DPS comparison sits alongside, with the rate and compounding basis printed next to the figure.

The four published constraints

each is a trap

Each of these is a place a plausible-looking implementation fails. These are the slides worth spending time on — they are what separates a demo from a solution.

C1An unsure field is shown as unsure — an amount is never guessed

The threshold is 0.8. At or above it a field is pre-filled and labelled with what was read. Below it the box renders empty, outlined in the reserved amber, saying it could not be read. Save stays disabled until all three fields are confirmed by hand, and the button counts them down.

The colour is the constraint

That amber — #b5730a — means one thing in this app and appears nowhere else. Two server guards back it up: a field failing validation is downgraded to “could not read”, and a null value can never carry a passing confidence.

C2The insights change when the numbers change

True by construction rather than by promise. Every line is a template over computed figures — there is no stored advice and no model-written text anywhere on that path. Each template either fires with real numbers or returns nothing.

The test harness proves it rather than asserting it: change one expense, and the page rewrites itself.

C3Pocket dates come from the forecast, not from division

There is no target / monthlyContribution anywhere in src/lib/pockets.ts. Division promises this person a wedding in 2027 that the arithmetic puts in 2034.

Why it matters

Across all 25 published cases the simulation puts 24 dates later than division would, and reports 15 pockets as not reachable at all rather than inventing a date for them.

C4The DPS rate, and how interest is added

8.00% a year, compounded monthly, deposit at the start of each month — printed on screen next to every figure, not only in the README, and editable because the published cases carry rates from 7.50% to 10.00%.

Each month: balance += deposit, then interest of balance × rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100 rounded half up to the paisa and added, so later months earn on it. All money in the app is an integer number of paisa — never a float — because that rule rounds every month and floating point would drift over a 190-month simulation.

The three published bonus features

all built

The brief allows these only once the four required items work. They do.

B1The what-if control

Cut one category by a percentage and every pocket completion date moves — on the same frame as the drag. No debounce, no memoisation: each drag event re-runs the whole forecast and the whole simulation from scratch, because they are pure functions over a few hundred records.

The cut applies to future spending only. Money already spent cannot be un-spent, which is why cutting Rent barely moves the month in progress but moves every month after.

B2Change a contribution, see every date move

A contribution slider on each pocket card, moving its own date as the thumb moves. The dates cross-fade in the direction they travelled — a later date rises, an earlier one drops — so the direction reads before the text does.

B3Automatic recurring detection

A shop billing in two consecutive months within 15% on the amount is marked recurring in the log and in the monthly-charges list.

The forecast uses a deliberately wider notion of a monthly charge than this badge does, since a utility bill recurs monthly while varying far more than 15%.

Beyond the brief

6 extra features

None of these is one of the three published bonuses. They were built after all four required items and all three bonuses were working.

X1Goal seek — the slider run backwards

The slider asks what a cut does to a date. This asks the question people actually have: “I want the Laptop by October 2027 — what has to be true?”

Nothing is modelled. For each candidate it binary-searches the cut percentage and runs the real forecast and the real simulation at every step, so every date shown is one the app would produce from the slider itself, and every cut is the smallest whole percent that works.

Measuring changed the design

Almost every deadline first came back “impossible” — the Laptop sits behind a ৳20,000-a-month Wedding pocket. That exposed the lever already in the app: the funding order is free. The solver now tries “fund this one first” before asking anyone to spend less.

X2Mobile money — paste a month at once

In Dhaka most spending never touches paper: it is a bKash, Nagad, Rocket or Upay confirmation. Those have a consistent shape, so they are read locally and deterministically — no model, no key, no network, no waiting.

It keeps the receipt screen's discipline. An unreadable amount goes amber and is never imported as a guess; money coming in is recognised and excluded with the count stated; an already-imported message is matched by transaction id; the category comes from your own history for that shop.

X3An assistant that can act, not just answer

Ask about your month, or just say what you spent. It can record an expense, create a pocket, change a contribution, run a what-if, load a sample case or open a screen.

Two properties make it safe next to someone's money. It never computes a figure — every request carries a digest built by the same forecast and simulation that draw the screens. And it cannot write to the ledger — it returns a typed, validated proposal rendered as an Apply / Discard card.

It refuses to invent

Asked for January 2025 it answers “I do not have your spending figures for January 2025” and offers what it does have. Told “I bought something at Agora” it asks for the amount rather than guessing.

X4Real Postgres persistence, without breaking the demo

localStorage stays the source of truth, because the live URL has to open and work with no setup — a required database would be a regression, not a feature. Backup is additive and opt-in: save once, get a ledger key, and changes push in the background; the same key restores everything on another device.

Security without accounts: row level security is on with no policies, so the publishable key can select nothing. The only surface is two security-definer functions that each require the ledger's own unguessable uuid.

X5All 25 published cases, loadable in the app

The organisers' fixture ships unmodified in public/sample-data. Setup → Show the 25 cases replaces the salary, expenses, pockets, DPS rate and the date being viewed with that case's own values.

That last one matters: every case carries its own today, so the app treats “today” as a setting rather than reading the clock. A case dated April 2026 judged on a machine whose clock says August would otherwise compute the entire forecast against the wrong month.

X6A verification harness that runs the real engine

npm run check runs the shipped forecast, pocket simulation and DPS arithmetic over all 25 published cases and asserts invariants that would catch a plausible-looking wrong answer — including that rent is never extrapolated past one month, and that the simulation never beats target ÷ contribution.

This is only possible because the engine has no React import anywhere: 2,381 lines of pure functions the browser and the terminal both run.

Decisions worth a slide of their own

technical execution
Integer paisa, never floats
The published DPS rule rounds half up every month; a float would drift across 190 months.
“Today” is a setting
Every published case carries its own date. Reading the clock would compute the wrong month.
A pure engine
2,381 lines with no React import — which is why the slider needs no debounce and the terminal can run the same code.
Seven runtime dependencies
Recharts and shadcn/ui were installed, went unused, and were removed rather than shipped dead.
Colour does one job
Money out, money in, and a reserved amber — checked with a palette validator at 11.4 ΔE under protanopia, all above 3:1.
Two layouts, one tree
A bottom bar on a phone, a fixed sidebar from lg up. Zero horizontal overflow at 360, 430, 1512 and 1920px.

What we would say if asked what is missing

stated, not hidden

Every one of these is already written into the README and the manifest. Saying them first is stronger than being asked.

No accounts

A backed-up ledger is protected only by its unguessable key — good enough to demonstrate persistence, not good enough for real money. Supabase Auth with RLS keyed on auth.uid() is the fix, and the client helpers are already wired for it.
